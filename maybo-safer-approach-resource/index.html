Why SAFER matters

Good decisions start by creating enough space to think.

Challenging situations can develop quickly. The SAFER Approach helps people avoid rushing into a response and instead consider the level of threat, the help available and the safest realistic options.

The model supports dynamic risk assessment rather than a single fixed decision.

The SAFER Approach

Five steps that support safer judgement.

Select a step to explore the practical thinking behind it.

01 / Step Back

Create distance before deciding.

Step back physically where possible, but also emotionally. A brief pause can reduce pressure and help you avoid reacting impulsively.

  • Use distance to improve safety
  • Slow your breathing and thinking
  • Avoid being drawn into confrontation

02 / Assess Threat

Read the whole situation, not just one signal.

Use Maybo’s POPS model to consider the people involved, nearby objects, the place and what is happening in the situation.

  • Notice behaviour and capability
  • Identify hazards and escape routes
  • Consider how quickly risk could change

03 / Find Help

Bring in support before options narrow.

Calling for help early can prevent escalation and provide more choices. Support may come from colleagues, security, emergency services or local procedures.

  • Use agreed alarm or call systems
  • Share clear, concise information
  • Avoid waiting until risk is critical

04 / Evaluate Options

Choose between intervention, observation, handover or withdrawal.

Consider what is realistic, lawful and safe. The best option may be to intervene, stand back and observe, hand over to someone else or move to safety.

  • Balance urgency with likely outcomes
  • Consider who is best placed to act
  • Keep withdrawal as a valid option

05 / Respond

Act proportionately and keep reassessing.

Use Maybo’s SEAL™ Test to guide your response. Continue monitoring until you are confident there is no longer a risk.

  • Use the least restrictive viable option
  • Stay alert to changes in threat
  • Review and adapt as new information appears

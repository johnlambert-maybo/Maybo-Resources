Strengthening confidence and safety in an Emergency Department

A tailored programme helped frontline staff build practical confidence in prevention, de-escalation, personal safety and proportionate physical responses.

See the outcomesExplore the programme
Real-world Emergency Department practice
Approach

A tailored programme for real clinical risk

Maybo combined preventative, relational and physical safety content so staff could select responses according to the person, the level of risk and the clinical context.

01 · Prevention

Positive Approaches to Behaviour

Understand behaviour, identify triggers and early warning signs, assess risk and choose responses that reduce escalation.

02 · De-escalation

Safer De-escalation

Use communication, active listening, safer positioning and calm responses to support people experiencing distress.

03 · Personal safety

Personal Safety and Disengagement

Maintain safer positioning and disengage from grips or holds when verbal approaches are insufficient.

04 · Proportionate response

Redirection and Guiding

Redirect or guide an unpredictable patient safely, with clear rationale and proportionality.

05 · Last resort

Clinical Holding

Use clinical holding only where necessary to protect the patient or others from harm.

Delivery

Training matched to role and exposure

The programme was shaped by a Training Needs Analysis and focused on realistic Emergency Department situations: mental health crisis, impaired capacity, unpredictable assault, high-stimulation environments and the tension between maintaining treatment and keeping everyone safe.

“The training was insightful. It showed us safer ways to support patients while being more mindful of the conditions and distress that may influence their behaviour.”Emergency Department staff feedback

Embedding learning

Refreshing skills over time

Periodic refresher activity can help maintain confidence, particularly for physical skills that may not be used frequently.

  • Build internal trainer capacity for consistent education, practice and reinforcement.
  • Align learning with local policy, governance and legal frameworks.
  • Use incident review to reinforce prevention and proportionality.
  • Keep patient dignity and staff safety at the centre of decision-making.

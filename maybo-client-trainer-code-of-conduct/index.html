Our commitment

Professional conduct with people at the centre

Every Maybo trainer is expected to uphold consistent standards of professionalism, model the principles they teach and place safety, dignity and respect at the heart of every learning experience.

People first

Prioritise the safety, dignity, rights and wellbeing of every individual.

Do no harm

Promote prevention, de-escalation and the reduction of restrictive practices.

Legal and proportionate

Teach practice that is lawful, necessary, proportionate and least restrictive.

Challenge and improve

Raise concerns about unsafe or poor practice and support continuous improvement.

Follow the programme

Deliver only authorised content, without unapproved changes, omissions or shortcuts.

Protect information

Respect confidentiality and safeguard personal, organisational and Maybo information.

Stay competent

Maintain current authorisation, skills, knowledge and professional development.

Act with integrity

Be honest, fair, objective and professional in all interactions and decisions.

What good practice looks like

The Code is intended to guide day-to-day decisions, not simply to sit alongside contractual documents.

Professional behaviour

  • Treat others with respect
  • Use inclusive language
  • Maintain professional boundaries
  • Challenge discrimination and harassment

Safe physical skills training

  • Teach progressively and safely
  • Supervise practice closely
  • Stop if conditions become unsafe
  • No full-force or improvised techniques

Safeguarding and duty of care

  • Follow safeguarding procedures
  • Report concerns promptly
  • Protect people from harm
  • Never retaliate against those who speak up

Records and accuracy

  • Keep complete, timely records
  • Never falsify or backdate
  • Report incidents and near misses
  • Record assessment outcomes honestly

Competence and development

  • Keep authorisation current
  • Complete refresher training
  • Engage with quality assurance
  • Declare factors affecting competence

Assessment integrity

  • Apply criteria consistently
  • Resist operational pressure
  • Declare conflicts of interest
  • Certify only demonstrated competence

01Purpose

This Code defines the professional, ethical and operational standards expected of trainers authorised to deliver Maybo programmes through a client Trainer Subscription.

  • Protect learners, colleagues and people receiving services.
  • Maintain the integrity, safety and quality of Maybo training.
  • Support lawful, ethical and proportionate practice.
  • Clarify trainer responsibilities where organisational expectations conflict with Maybo standards.
02Scope

The Code applies whenever a trainer delivers, assesses or supports a Maybo programme; represents themselves as Maybo-qualified; uses Maybo intellectual property; advises colleagues on Maybo-related practice; or records and verifies Maybo training activity. It applies to face-to-face, virtual and blended delivery.

03Core professional commitment
  • Act with integrity, professionalism and sound judgement.
  • Place safety, dignity, rights and wellbeing at the centre of practice.
  • Support prevention, de-escalation and reduction of restrictive practice.
  • Ensure physical intervention content is framed by legality, necessity, proportionality and least restriction.
  • Challenge unsafe, unlawful, discriminatory or degrading practice.
  • Apply the higher standard where requirements differ, unless doing so would conflict with law.
04Authority to deliver Maybo training

Trainers must deliver only programmes, modules, learner groups and operational settings within their current authorisation and the client’s active licence.

  • Do not create unofficial “Maybo-based” or hybrid programmes.
  • Do not add, remove or substitute techniques or assessment criteria.
  • Do not teach content from other providers as part of a Maybo programme.
  • Clearly distinguish Maybo content, law, local policy and personal professional opinion.
05Fidelity to the Maybo programme

Programmes must be delivered in line with current presentations, session plans, trainer guidance, learner materials, risk-reduction guidance, assessment requirements, venue requirements, durations and ratios.

Required learning must not be compressed, omitted or treated as optional because of operational pressure, learner seniority or managerial request.

06Person-centred and rights-respecting practice

Training must respect dignity, autonomy, communication, protected characteristics, culture, trauma history, sensory and cognitive needs, and physical and psychological wellbeing.

  • Avoid labels that blame or dehumanise people.
  • Do not normalise restrictive intervention as routine, punitive or a substitute for adequate staffing and support.
  • Emphasise prevention, planning and least-restrictive responses.
07Safeguarding and duty to raise concerns

Trainers must remain alert to abuse, neglect, excessive restraint, degrading treatment, unlawful restriction, discrimination, falsified records, unsafe staffing, concealment or retaliation.

Concerns must be raised through suitable safeguarding, whistleblowing or incident-reporting routes, and Maybo must be informed where concerns materially relate to misuse or misrepresentation of Maybo training.

08Safe management of the training environment
  • Confirm the venue is suitable and free from avoidable hazards.
  • Check space, emergency arrangements, first aid, equipment and trainer-to-learner ratios.
  • Consider reasonable adjustments and privacy needs.
  • Stop, modify or postpone activity where safe delivery is not possible.
09Learner suitability and participation

Learners must have a meaningful opportunity to identify health, mobility, pregnancy, trauma, disability, cultural or other relevant needs.

Participation may be restricted where necessary for safety. Learners may be excluded for threatening or discriminatory behaviour, intoxication, repeated disruption, failure to complete mandatory learning, insufficient attendance or conduct incompatible with safe practice.

10Physical skills training
  • Teach progressively through explanation, demonstration, practice, observation and correction.
  • Explain stop signals and safety principles before practice.
  • Prevent competitive, punitive or excessively forceful practice.
  • Stop immediately where pain, distress, panic or unsafe conduct occurs.
  • Do not improvise unauthorised techniques or deliver informal demonstrations.
11Assessment integrity

Assessment must be fair, objective, evidence-based and free from organisational pressure.

  • Only confirm successful completion where attendance, participation, knowledge, practical competence and relevant attitudes meet Maybo criteria.
  • Do not pass a learner because of seniority, staffing pressure, cost or convenience.
  • Declare conflicts of interest and seek suitable oversight.
12Professional boundaries and behaviour
  • Do not bully, intimidate, humiliate or ridicule learners.
  • Do not use discriminatory, sexual or offensive language.
  • Do not use physical skills to establish authority or dominance.
  • Do not exploit trainer status or form inappropriate relationships.
  • Do not deliver while impaired by alcohol, drugs, medication or fatigue.
  • Use humour carefully and never trivialise trauma, restraint, disability, injury or death.
13Equality, diversity, inclusion and reasonable adjustment

Provide an inclusive environment and consider adjustments that improve access without undermining essential learning or safety requirements.

Adjustments may include adapted communication, processing time, accessible materials, modified positioning or alternative methods of demonstrating knowledge. Practical competence must still be demonstrated before certification.

14Confidentiality, data protection and information security
  • Access information only where necessary and use approved systems.
  • Keep attendance, assessment and incident records secure.
  • Anonymise real examples wherever practicable.
  • Report suspected data breaches promptly.
  • Do not upload learner data, confidential information or Maybo materials into unapproved AI tools or storage systems.
15Intellectual property and use of Maybo materials

Maybo materials may only be used for authorised delivery by approved trainers within the client’s active subscription.

  • Do not copy, sell, publish, distribute or record materials without permission.
  • Do not upload resources to public or unauthorised platforms.
  • Do not remove copyright or trademark notices.
  • Keep access credentials confidential.
16Records and reporting

Records must be complete, accurate, timely and legible. This includes attendance, preparatory learning, assessment, adjustments, exclusions, incidents, evaluation and trainer reflection.

Serious matters must be escalated immediately. Records must never be falsified, backdated or altered to conceal non-compliance.

17Competence and continuing professional development
  • Complete required refreshers and reassessment.
  • Review updates before further delivery.
  • Maintain enough delivery activity or practice to remain competent.
  • Participate in observation, quality assurance and standardisation.
  • Declare illness, injury or other factors affecting competence.
18Quality assurance and cooperation

Trainers must cooperate with observation, record review, learner feedback, moderation, reassessment, investigation and corrective action. Information must be honest and complete, and learners must not be coached to provide misleading feedback.

19Conflicts of interest and organisational pressure

Trainers must disclose conflicts affecting assessment, incident reporting, selection of learners or escalation of concerns.

Operational or managerial pressure must not compromise programme duration, learner numbers, assessment, record accuracy, safety, safeguarding or authorised content. Persistent pressure must be escalated and may require the trainer to decline delivery or certification.

20Social media and public communication
  • Do not imply that personal statements are official Maybo statements.
  • Describe trainer status accurately and protect confidentiality.
  • Do not publish training images or recordings without appropriate permissions.
  • Do not demonstrate Maybo intellectual property publicly without written consent.
21Complaints, concerns and investigations

Learners must know how to raise concerns about trainers, learners, content, safety, discrimination, safeguarding and assessment.

Trainers must cooperate fully with investigations and must not influence witnesses, coordinate accounts, alter evidence, contact complainants inappropriately or make misleading statements.

22Breaches of this Code

Potential outcomes include advice, supervision, an improvement plan, reassessment, suspension, restriction of scope, removal of authorisation, notification to the client, subscription action, or referral to an employer, regulator, safeguarding authority or law-enforcement body.

The response will consider seriousness, actual or potential harm, intent, previous concerns, honesty, remediation and risk of recurrence.

23Trainer declaration

Trainers confirm that they have read and understood the Code, will deliver only within current authorisation, protect Maybo programmes and materials, report relevant concerns, participate in quality assurance and notify the appropriate person if they cannot meet these requirements.
